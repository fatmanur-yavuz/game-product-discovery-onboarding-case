Feature PRD: Clear Early Progression (MVP)

Type: Product Case Study / Hypothesis
Role Focus: Product Specialist / Associate Product Manager
Context: Hypothetical mobile puzzle game; no real company data used.

---

1. Overview

Clear Early Progression is a lightweight feature concept designed to help new players understand their immediate goal, recognize their progress, and anticipate their next milestone.

The MVP introduces a visual progress indicator, a clearly communicated next goal, and one early milestone reward.

Problem: New players may struggle to recognize their progress during early gameplay.

Product Hypothesis: Making early progress and the next milestone more visible may improve first-session activation without adding unnecessary friction.

2. MVP Scope

| In Scope | Out of Scope |
| :--- | :--- |
| One progress indicator in the main UI | Multi-tier achievement systems |
| One early milestone reward using soft currency | Social sharing or guild progression |
| Clear next-goal communication | Complex monetization integration |
| Progress feedback after the first meaningful player action | Advanced personalization |

3. Success Metrics and Guardrails

Primary Metric — Early Player Activation Rate

The percentage of new players who complete a predefined meaningful early-game action within their first session.

Secondary Metric — D1 Retention

The percentage of new players who return on Day 1, using a consistent cohort definition.

Guardrail Metric — Tutorial Abandonment Rate

The feature should not cause a meaningful increase in tutorial abandonment.

Illustrative Experiment Criterion: A relative improvement of 5% in activation could be used as an initial evaluation threshold. This is a hypothetical planning assumption, not a data-backed forecast. The final decision should also consider sample size, statistical uncertainty, implementation cost, and D1 retention.

4. Risks and Mitigations

Risk: Visual clutter during early gameplay.
Mitigation: Keep the progress indicator minimal and introduce it only after the first meaningful player action.

Risk: Players focus on the reward instead of understanding the game.
Mitigation: Make the next goal understandable without requiring players to interpret multiple reward systems.

Risk: Activation improves but D1 retention does not.
Mitigation: Evaluate short-term activation alongside return behavior before deciding whether to expand the feature.

5. Decision Principle

The feature should be expanded only if the experiment indicates that it improves early player progression without introducing unacceptable friction or harming subsequent retention.
