# Product Decision Framework (Hypothetical Case)

This document outlines how experiment results could inform the next steps for the Clear Early Progression feature in a hypothetical mobile puzzle game.

No experiment has been conducted. The scenarios below are illustrative examples of possible outcomes, not actual results.

---

## 1. Experiment Objective

Evaluate whether making early progress and the next goal more visible helps new players progress through the first session without introducing unnecessary friction.

---

## 2. Hypothesis

If new players receive clearer progress feedback and a visible next goal, they may be more likely to complete a predefined early-game action.

The feature should improve early progression without meaningfully increasing tutorial abandonment or harming Day 1 retention.

---

## 3. Experiment Design

Control Group (A): Players experience the existing onboarding and progression flow.

Test Group (B): Players experience the proposed progress indicator, next-goal communication, and early milestone reward.

New players would be assigned to the groups using a consistent randomization method.

Before the experiment begins, the team should define the activation event, target audience, experiment duration, sample size, and decision criteria.

### *Metrics*

* *Primary Metric:* Early Player Activation Rate — the percentage of new players who complete the predefined early-game action during their first session.
* *Secondary Metric:* D1 Retention — the percentage of new players who return on Day 1.
* *Guardrail Metric:* Tutorial Abandonment Rate — the percentage of players who leave during the tutorial.

---

## 4. Possible Outcomes and Decisions

### *Scenario A: Positive Outcome*

* *Illustrative result:* Activation improves, tutorial abandonment does not meaningfully increase, and D1 retention remains stable or improves.
* *Potential decision:* Consider expanding the feature gradually, while continuing to monitor player behavior.
* *Follow-up question:* Does the improvement remain consistent across relevant player segments?

### *Scenario B: Mixed Outcome*

* *Illustrative result:* Activation improves, but D1 retention declines or tutorial abandonment increases.
* *Potential decision:* Investigate where the additional friction occurs before expanding the feature. Consider simplifying the progress indicator, adjusting when it appears, or revisiting the reward.
* *Follow-up question:* Is the feature encouraging short-term completion without supporting a satisfying gameplay experience?

### *Scenario C: No Meaningful Improvement*

* *Illustrative result:* Activation shows no meaningful improvement, and the guardrail metrics remain broadly unchanged.
* *Potential decision:* Reassess whether progress visibility is the main problem. Review player feedback and behavioral data before investing in a more complex solution.
* *Follow-up question:* Do players need clearer goals, more engaging early gameplay, or a different form of guidance?

---

## 5. Decision Principles

The team should not make a decision based on one metric alone.

Before interpreting the results, it should consider:

* The size and reliability of the observed effect.
* Sample size and statistical uncertainty.
* Changes in the primary, secondary, and guardrail metrics.
* Implementation and maintenance costs.
* Potential differences between player segments.

A positive result would support further investigation, but it would not automatically prove that the feature is the only cause of improved player behavior.

---

## 6. Final Decision Framework

* *Expand:* Evidence supports the hypothesis, and guardrail metrics remain acceptable.
* *Iterate:* Results are mixed or indicate a fixable usability issue.
* *Reassess:* Results do not support the hypothesis, or evidence suggests the underlying problem was misunderstood.

The final decision should depend on the experiment results and their reliability, not on a predetermined preference for the feature.

---

Important Note: This is a hypothetical decision framework. No real experiment, player data, or measured outcome is claimed.
